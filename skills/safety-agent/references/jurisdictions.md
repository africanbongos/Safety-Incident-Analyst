# Jurisdictions reference: duties, notification, records, psychosocial

Verified 16 Oct 2026 against primary or regulator sources (URLs at the end of each section). "(verify)" means the point is well established in practice but was not confirmed against a primary source during this research. This is a reference aid, not legal advice. Always check the current version of the law in the relevant jurisdiction.

---

## 1. Model WHS laws (Commonwealth model, adopted by all jurisdictions except Victoria)

**Laws and adoption**
- Model: Work Health and Safety Act 2011 and model WHS Regulations, maintained by Safe Work Australia. The model has legal effect only when each jurisdiction enacts it.
- NSW: WHS Act 2011 (NSW) + WHS Regulation 2025 (commenced 22 Aug 2025, replaced the 2017 Regulation). Regulator: SafeWork NSW (standalone regulator from 1 Jul 2025).
- QLD: WHS Act 2011 (Qld) + WHS Regulation 2011 (Qld). Regulator: Workplace Health and Safety Queensland (verify). Coal and metalliferous mines sit under separate mining safety Acts (verify).
- SA: WHS Act 2012 (SA) + WHS Regulations 2012 (SA). Regulator: SafeWork SA.
- TAS: WHS Act 2012 (Tas) + WHS Regulations 2022 (Tas). Regulator: WorkSafe Tasmania.
- ACT: WHS Act 2011 (ACT) + WHS Regulation 2011 (ACT). Regulator: WorkSafe ACT.
- NT: WHS (National Uniform Legislation) Act 2011 + Regulations 2011. Regulator: NT WorkSafe.
- WA: Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA), commenced 31 Mar 2022, with three sets of regulations: WHS (General) Regulations 2022, WHS (Mines) Regulations 2022, WHS (Petroleum and Geothermal Energy Operations) Regulations 2022. Regulator: the WorkSafe Commissioner. WA has an industrial manslaughter offence (PCBUs and officers) and Category 1/2/3 offences. Major hazard facilities and dangerous goods stay under the Dangerous Goods Safety Act 2004 (WA).
- Commonwealth: WHS Act 2011 (Cth) + WHS Regulations 2011 (Cth). Regulator: Comcare (Commonwealth agencies and self-insured licensees).

**Duty-holder terminology**
- PCBU (person conducting a business or undertaking) owes the primary duty of care (s19) to ensure health and safety "so far as is reasonably practicable" (s18 defines reasonably practicable).
- Officer (s27): due diligence duty. Worker (s7): broad, includes contractors, labour hire, volunteers. Other persons at the workplace also have duties.

**Notifiable incident (s35)**: death of a person, a serious injury or illness (s36), or a dangerous incident (s37), arising out of the conduct of the business or undertaking. Applies to workers and non-workers.

**Serious injury or illness (s36)**: an injury or illness requiring:
- immediate treatment as an in-patient in a hospital (any length of stay); or
- immediate treatment for: amputation of any part of the body; serious head injury; serious eye injury; serious burn; separation of skin from underlying tissue (degloving, scalping); spinal injury; loss of a bodily function; serious lacerations; or
- medical treatment within 48 hours of exposure to a substance;
- plus prescribed serious illnesses (work-related infections and listed zoonoses such as Q fever, anthrax, leptospirosis, brucellosis, Hendra virus, avian influenza, psittacosis; model reg 699 (verify reg number)).

**Dangerous incident (s37)**: an incident that exposes a worker or any other person to a serious risk from immediate or imminent exposure to:
- uncontrolled escape, spillage or leakage of a substance
- uncontrolled implosion, explosion or fire
- uncontrolled escape of gas or steam, or of a pressurised substance
- electric shock (excludes static, extra-low voltage, deliberate defibrillator use, per NSW guidance)
- fall or release from height of any plant, substance or thing
- collapse, overturning, failure or malfunction of, or damage to, plant that must be authorised (registered)
- collapse or partial collapse of a structure
- collapse or failure of an excavation or its shoring
- inrush of water, mud or gas in underground workings or a tunnel
- interruption of the main ventilation system in underground workings or a tunnel
- any other prescribed event.

**Notification (s38)**
- PCBU must ensure the regulator is notified **immediately after becoming aware**; "by the fastest possible means", by telephone or in writing (including electronic).
- If the regulator requires it after a phone notice, written notice must be given **within 48 hours of the requirement**.
- Practical: NSW treats the business as aware once any supervisor or manager knows; SafeWork NSW line 13 10 50 (24/7). Notify even without full details.

**Site preservation (s39)**: the person with management or control of the workplace must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the site is not disturbed until an inspector arrives or directs otherwise. Exceptions: assisting an injured person, removing a deceased person, making the site safe or preventing a further incident, police investigation, regulator or inspector permission. Only the incident area, not the whole workplace. Inspectors can issue a non-disturbance notice of up to 7 days.

**Records**: keep a record of each notifiable incident for **at least 5 years** from the day notice is given (s38).

**Penalties (NSW example)**: failure to notify up to 121 penalty units (individual) and 607 (body corporate) per the current NSW Act text.

**Reform pipeline (not yet law unless adopted locally)**: WHS ministers approved, and Safe Work Australia published (Dec 2025), the *Model WHS Legislation Amendment (Incident Notification) 2025*. It would:
- add notifiable **violent incidents** (sexual assault, physical assault, unlawful deprivation of liberty, credible threats) where there is a serious risk of psychological harm (new s37A);
- add notifiable **extended absences** of 15 or more consecutive days linked to a physical or psychological work-related injury or illness, notify within 14 days (new s35A);
- add notifiable **work-related suicides and attempts** (new s35B);
- rewrite s36 (adds serious brain injury from blows to the head; removes the "medical treatment" definition) and s37 (adds mobile plant incidents and serious falls);
- extend s39 to preserving evidence (including electronic records, witness details) and add mutual notification between PCBU and workplace manager (s39A).
Safe Work Australia states these "do not apply unless adopted in your jurisdiction". As at Oct 2026 no adoption was confirmed in this research; check the local regulator.

**Psychosocial hazards**: model WHS Regulations regs 55A to 55D require PCBUs to manage psychosocial risks using the risk management process and hierarchy of controls. Commencement by jurisdiction: NSW 1 Oct 2022; TAS 12 Dec 2022; WA 24 Dec 2022; Cth 1 Apr 2023; QLD 1 Apr 2023 (with a Managing the Risk of Psychosocial Hazards at Work Code of Practice); NT 1 Jul 2023; ACT 27 Nov 2023; SA 25 Dec 2023. NSW 2025 Regulation reinforces that policies, procedures and EAPs alone are not enough. NSW Workplace Protections Amendment Act 2025 adds six-monthly psychosocial reporting requirements from 1 Mar 2026 (details of who reports what: verify) and a duty to comply with approved codes of practice (or an equivalent or higher standard) from 1 Jul 2026.

**Enforcement focus**: Australian WHS Strategy 2023-2033 national targets: cut traumatic fatalities 30%, cut serious claims frequency rate (1+ week off) 20%, cut permanent impairment claims frequency 15%, work-related injury or illness incidence below 3.5%, cut respiratory disease frequency 20%, no new accelerated silicosis cases by 2033.

## 2. Victoria: Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004

**Laws and regulator**: OHS Act 2004 (Vic), OHS Regulations 2017 (Vic) (verify current version), OHS (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025. Regulator: WorkSafe Victoria.

**Terminology**: "employer" and "self-employed person" (not PCBU). Employers must provide and maintain a working environment that is safe and without risks to health "so far as is reasonably practicable" (s21; s20 sets out what "reasonably practicable" means) (verify section numbers). Officers have their own duty (verify section). "Employee", not "worker".

**Notifiable incidents (s37)** (employer or self-employed person with management or control of the workplace):
- Death.
- A person needing medical treatment within 48 hours of exposure to a substance.
- A person needing immediate treatment as an in-patient in a hospital.
- A person needing immediate medical treatment for: amputation; serious head injury; serious eye injury; degloving or scalping; **electric shock** (Vic lists this as an injury, excluding static and deliberate defibrillator use); spinal injury (not muscular); loss of a bodily function; serious lacerations.
- Dangerous incidents exposing a person to immediate or imminent serious risk: uncontrolled escape, spillage or leakage of any substance (including dangerous goods); implosion, explosion or fire; electric shock; fall or release from height of plant, substance or thing; collapse, overturning, failure, malfunction of or damage to listed plant (cranes and lifting plant, pressure equipment, tractors, earthmoving machinery, scaffolds, temporary access equipment, explosive-powered tools, turbines, amusement structures, registrable-design plant); collapse or partial collapse of a building or structure; collapse or failure of an excavation, mine or shoring; inrush of water, mud or gas in a mine, underground excavation or tunnel; interruption of main ventilation in a mine, underground excavation or tunnel; other prescribed events.
- If the only person harmed is the employer or self-employed person themselves, notification is not required.

**Notification (s38)**: phone WorkSafe **immediately** after becoming aware (13 23 60, 24/7); **written notification within 48 hours** (this is automatic in Vic, unlike model WHS where written notice is only on request). Dangerous goods fires, explosions, spills or leaks must also be reported immediately to the fire authority or police under the Dangerous Goods Act 1985.

**Site preservation (s39)**: do not disturb the site until an inspector arrives or directs otherwise, unless needed to protect health and safety, aid an injured person, or take essential action to make the site safe or prevent a further incident. Inspectors can issue a non-disturbance notice of up to 7 days.

**Records**: keep a copy of the written notification for **at least 5 years**; must be available to inspectors, injured or exposed persons and their representatives, the relevant HSR and health and safety committee members.

**Psychosocial hazards**: Occupational Health and Safety (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025 commenced **1 Dec 2025** for all Victorian employers, with a Compliance Code: Psychological Health. Employers must identify psychosocial hazards, eliminate risks so far as reasonably practicable, otherwise reduce them by altering work design, systems of work, management of work, plant or environment; information, instruction or training may be used alone only where nothing else is reasonably practicable and cannot be the predominant control. Review triggers include a report of psychological injury or hazard and a notifiable incident involving a psychosocial hazard. Prevention plans are optional (WorkSafe template). Hazards named include bullying, sexual harassment, aggression or violence, traumatic events or content, high job demands, low job control.

**Enforcement focus**: psychological health regulations implementation (2025-26) (verify specific WorkSafe priorities).

Sources: https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/notifiable-incidents-under-occupational-health-and-safety-act-2004 ; https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/summary-ohs-act-2004-incidents ; https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/new-regulations-start-today-protect-workers ; https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/psychological-health-regulations-frequently-asked-questions

## 3. New Zealand: Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA)

**Laws and regulator**: HSWA 2015 plus regulations (e.g. General Risk and Workplace Management Regulations 2016, Mining and Quarrying Regulations 2016, Hazardous Substances Regulations 2017) (verify full list). Regulator: WorkSafe New Zealand (Maritime NZ for maritime and, from 1 Jul 2024, the 13 major ports; CAA for aviation).

**Terminology**: PCBU (s17), officer (s18), worker (s19), workplace (s20); primary duty of care s36, "so far as is reasonably practicable" (s22). Officers' due diligence (s44).

**Notifiable event (s25)**: death, notifiable injury or illness (s23), or notifiable incident (s24), arising from work.

**Notifiable injury or illness (s23)**: any injury or illness requiring (or that would usually require) admission to hospital for immediate treatment; amputation; serious head injury; serious eye injury; serious burn; degloving or scalping; spinal injury (not back strain); loss of bodily function; serious lacerations; medical treatment within 48 hours of exposure to a substance; serious infection (including occupational zoonoses) where work is a significant contributing factor; anything declared by regulations.

**Notifiable incident (s24)**: unplanned or uncontrolled incident that exposes a worker or other person to a serious risk from immediate or imminent exposure to: escape, spillage or leakage of a substance; implosion, explosion or fire; escape of gas, steam or pressurised substance; electric shock (potentially lethal); fall or release from height of plant, substance or thing; damage, collapse, overturning, failure or malfunction of plant declared by regulations; collapse or partial collapse of a structure; collapse or failure of an excavation or shoring; inrush of water, mud or gas, or interruption of ventilation, in underground workings; collision, capsize or inrush of water of a vessel; plus regulation-declared incidents (adventure activities, asbestos, MHFs, mining and quarrying, petroleum). Planned and controlled activities are excluded.

**Notification (s56)**: "as soon as possible" after becoming aware; "by the fastest possible means in the circumstances", by telephone or in writing (including email or electronic). If notified by phone and the regulator requires it, written notice **within 48 hours** of being told. Deaths: phone WorkSafe 0800 030 040 (24/7); other events via the online Notify WorkSafe tool. One notification per event, though all PCBUs involved share responsibility. Penalty for failing to notify: up to NZD 10,000 (individual), NZD 50,000 (other).

**Site preservation (s55)**: PCBU managing or controlling the workplace must ensure, so far as reasonably practicable, the site is not disturbed until an inspector permits. Exceptions: assisting the injured, removing the deceased, making safe, police direction, regulator or inspector permission. Work can continue elsewhere.

**Records (s57)**: keep a record of each notifiable event for **at least 5 years** from the date notice was given.

**Reform**: Health and Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026 (Royal Assent Jul 2026) commences **1 Apr 2027**. Introduces "critical risks" (Schedule 1A plus any hazard likely to cause death, notifiable injury or illness, notifiable incident or Schedule 2 ACC occupational disease), lighter obligations for small PCBUs (fewer than 20 workers), clarified officer due diligence (governance capacity), and ACOP safe harbour. Notification duties are reported as clarified; specific changes not verified (verify). Until then the current Act applies in full. Politically contested (repeal pledged by Labour).

**Psychosocial hazards**: no psychosocial-specific regulations. HSWA "health" includes mental health; WorkSafe "Managing psychosocial risks at work" guidelines (last updated 10 Apr 2025) apply the s36 primary duty, s30 risk management, s34 overlapping duties and Part 3 engagement. Under the 2026 amendment a psychosocial hazard can be a critical risk if it is likely to cause a notifiable mental-health injury or illness (from 1 Apr 2027).

## 4. United States: OSH Act 1970, 29 CFR Part 1904 (federal OSHA)

**Laws and regulator**: Occupational Safety and Health Act 1970; recordkeeping and reporting at 29 CFR Part 1904; standards at Parts 1910 (general industry) and 1926 (construction). Regulator: OSHA (Department of Labor); about half the states run OSHA-approved State Plans with equal or stricter rules (verify per state). Duty holder: "employer"; General Duty Clause (OSH Act s5(a)(1)) requires a workplace free from recognized hazards likely to cause death or serious physical harm (verify wording). No "reasonably practicable" qualifier; "feasibility" concepts apply in standards.

**Who must keep records**: employers with more than 10 employees at any time in the prior calendar year (company-wide count), unless in a partially exempt industry. Exempt employers must still report severe injuries (1904.1).

**Recordable case (1904.7(b)(1))**: a work-related (1904.5) new case (1904.6) resulting in any of: death; days away from work; restricted work or job transfer; medical treatment beyond first aid; loss of consciousness; or a significant injury or illness diagnosed by a physician or other licensed health care professional (1904.7(b)(7): cancer, chronic irreversible disease, fractured or cracked bone, punctured eardrum). Special cases: needlesticks (1904.8), medical removal (1904.9), hearing loss (1904.10), TB (1904.11).
- First aid (1904.7(b)(5)(ii)) is a closed list: non-prescription medication at non-prescription strength; tetanus shots; cleaning or soaking surface wounds; bandages, butterfly strips, Steri-Strips; hot or cold therapy; non-rigid supports; temporary immobilisation for transport; draining blisters, drilling nails; eye patches; eye foreign body removal by irrigation or cotton swab; splinter removal by simple means; finger guards; massage; drinking fluids for heat stress. Anything else (sutures, staples, rigid splints, prescription meds, physical therapy) is medical treatment.
- Day counting: calendar days starting the day after injury, including weekends and holidays; cap at 180 days (1904.7(b)(3)).
- Mental illness is not work-related unless the employee voluntarily provides a licensed health care professional's opinion that it is (1904.5(b)(2)(ix)).
- Enter on the 300 Log and 301 within **7 calendar days** of learning of a recordable case (1904.29(b)(3)).

**Forms**: OSHA 300 (Log of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses), 300A (Annual Summary), 301 (Injury and Illness Incident Report).

**Posting (1904.32)**: post the certified 300A no later than **Feb 1** of the following year and keep it posted until **Apr 30**.

**Retention (1904.33)**: **5 years** following the end of the calendar year the records cover (300 Log must be updated during that period).

**Electronic submission (1904.41)**, due **Mar 2** each year for the prior year:
- 300A: establishments with 250+ employees (that must keep records); and establishments with 20-249 employees in Appendix A industries.
- 300 and 301: establishments with 100+ employees in Appendix B industries.
- Excluded fields: employee name (300); employee name and address, health care professional name, treatment facility name and address (301).

**Severe injury reporting (1904.39)**:
- Fatality: within **8 hours** of learning of the death (only if death occurs within 30 days of the incident).
- In-patient hospitalization (formal admission for care or treatment, not observation or diagnostic testing only), amputation (traumatic loss of a limb or other external body part; excludes avulsions, deglovings, scalpings, severed ears, broken teeth), or loss of an eye: within **24 hours** (only if within 24 hours of the incident).
- Methods: phone or in person to nearest OSHA Area Office; 1-800-321-OSHA (6742); or OSHA online reporting app. No voicemail, fax or email.
- Not reportable to OSHA (but still recordable): motor vehicle accidents on a public street or highway outside a construction work zone; incidents on commercial or public transport (plane, train, bus, subway). Heart attacks at work must be reported (area office decides whether to inspect).
- Clock starts when the employer or its agent learns of the event or its work-relatedness.

**Site preservation**: no general Part 1904 site-preservation duty (verify; OSHA may request preservation during inspection).

**Psychosocial hazards**: no federal OSHA standard on psychosocial risk, workplace stress or general workplace violence (verify current rulemaking); OSHA has used the General Duty Clause in workplace violence cases (verify).

**Enforcement focus**: current National Emphasis Programs (OSHA NEP page): Heat (CPL 03-00-024, updated 10 Apr 2026), Warehousing and Distribution Centers (CPL 03-00-026, 31 Jul 2026), Hazardous Machinery / Amputations in Manufacturing (CPL 03-00-027, 26 Jun 2025), Falls (CPL 03-00-025), Trenching and Excavation, Silica, Combustible Dust, PSM, Lead, Hexavalent Chromium, Primary Metals, Shipbreaking.

## 5. United Kingdom (Great Britain): HSWA 1974 + RIDDOR 2013

**Laws and regulator**: Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974; Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 (risk assessment, reg 3) (verify); Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR, SI 2013/1471). Regulator: HSE, with local authorities enforcing in many retail, office, leisure and hospitality premises. Northern Ireland has separate legislation and HSENI (verify).

**Terminology**: duty holders, chiefly the "employer" (HSWA s2 duty to employees, s3 to non-employees, "so far as is reasonably practicable"). HSE uses ALARP ("as low as reasonably practicable") as the equivalent risk test. RIDDOR duties fall on the "responsible person" (reg 3), usually the employer or person in control of premises.

**Reportable under RIDDOR**:
- Work-related deaths of workers and non-workers (reg 6), excluding suicides; also a worker death within one year of a reportable injury.
- Specified injuries to workers (reg 4(1)): fracture diagnosed by a doctor other than to fingers, thumbs or toes; amputation of an arm, hand, finger, thumb, leg, foot or toe; injury likely to cause permanent blinding or reduction in sight; crush injury to head or torso damaging brain or internal organs; burn (including scald) covering more than 10% of body surface or significantly damaging eyes, respiratory system or vital organs; any scalping requiring hospital treatment; loss of consciousness from head injury or asphyxia; enclosed-space injury leading to hypothermia or heat-induced illness, or requiring resuscitation or hospital admission for more than 24 hours.
- Over-7-day incapacitation of a worker (reg 4(2)): unable to do routine work for more than 7 consecutive days, not counting the day of the accident.
- Non-workers (reg 5): injured in connection with work and taken directly from the scene to hospital for treatment (examinations and X-rays alone do not count; precautionary trips do not count).
- Occupational diseases (reg 8) on written diagnosis: carpal tunnel syndrome, hand or forearm cramp, occupational dermatitis, HAVS, occupational asthma, hand or forearm tendonitis or tenosynovitis; plus carcinogen, mutagen and biological agent exposures (reg 9).
- Dangerous occurrences (reg 7, Schedule 2), e.g. collapse, overturning or load-bearing failure of lifting equipment (para 1); failure of a pressure system with potential to cause death (para 2); contact with or arcing from uninsulated overhead lines above 200 V (para 3); electrical short circuit or overload causing fire or explosion with over 24 hours out of action or potential to cause death (para 4); release of serious biological agents (para 10); breathing apparatus malfunction (para 12); collapse of scaffold over 5 m (para 18); unintended collapse of a structure under construction, alteration or demolition (more than 5 tonnes) or of falsework (paras 23, 24); fire or explosion stopping work over 24 hours (para 25); release of flammable liquids or gases (para 26); hazardous escape of substances (para 27). Separate lists for mines, quarries, rail, offshore and pipelines.
- Gas incidents (reg 11): for gas suppliers and Gas Safe registered engineers.

**Timeframes and method** (Schedule 1 Part 1 para 1):
- Deaths, specified injuries, non-worker hospital cases, dangerous occurrences: notify "by the quickest practicable means without delay" and send a report **within 10 days**.
- Over-7-day injuries: report **within 15 days** of the accident.
- Occupational diseases: as soon as the responsible person receives a diagnosis.
- Methods: online forms for everything; telephone (0345 300 9923) only for fatal and specified injuries.

**Site preservation**: RIDDOR has no general site-preservation duty (reg 13 covers mines, quarries and offshore only). HSE inspectors can direct that premises or anything in them be left undisturbed (HSWA s20(2)(e)).

**Records (reg 12)**: record all reportable incidents and diagnoses plus over-3-day injuries (incapacitated more than 3 consecutive days, excluding accident day); keep for **at least 3 years** from the date of entry.

**Psychosocial hazards**: no specific psychosocial regulations. Employers must assess the risk of work-related stress as part of the general risk assessment duty (verify reg 3 MHSWR 1999 citation). HSE Management Standards cover six areas: Demands, Control, Support, Relationships, Role, Change; HSE says following the approach correctly is "suitable and sufficient".

**Enforcement and context**: HSE key figures: 126 workers killed in 2025/26 (RIDDOR, provisional figure as labelled by HSE); 964,000 workers with work-related stress, depression or anxiety; 59,219 employer-reported (RIDDOR) non-fatal injuries to employees (latest HSE overview). Specific HSE enforcement priorities not verified.

## Quick notifiability triage table

"Model WHS" = current law in NSW, QLD, SA, TAS, ACT, NT, WA, Cth (pre-2025-amendment wording). Always also check the in-patient test: in AU and NZ, any immediate hospital admission is notifiable regardless of injury type.

| Event type | Model WHS (s35-37) | Vic (s37) | NZ (s23-25) | US OSHA (1904.39) | UK RIDDOR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Death (work-related, any person) | Notify immediately | Notify immediately; written within 48 h | Notify ASAP; phone WorkSafe | Employees only: report within 8 h (death within 30 days of incident) | Report without delay, written within 10 days (workers and non-workers; not suicide) |
| Amputation | Yes, any part of body | Yes (needing immediate medical treatment) | Yes (needing more than first aid) | Yes, within 24 h (traumatic loss of external body part; excludes degloving, avulsion) | Specified injury if arm, hand, finger, thumb, leg, foot or toe |
| Serious burn | Yes if serious (e.g. needing intensive or critical care) | Not listed separately; notifiable if in-patient admission (verify) | Yes if needing intensive or critical care (e.g. skin graft) | Only if in-patient hospitalization | Specified if over 10% body surface or damaging eyes, respiratory system, vital organs |
| Hospital in-patient admission | Yes, any length of stay | Yes | Yes (admission for immediate treatment) | Yes, within 24 h (treatment, not observation) | Workers: only if a specified injury or over 7 days; non-workers: taken directly to hospital for treatment |
| Eye injury | Serious eye injury (e.g. loss of vision, penetrating object) | Serious eye injury | Serious eye injury likely to cause loss of eye or vision | Loss of an eye: within 24 h | Injury likely to cause permanent blinding or reduced sight |
| Fracture | Only if it meets s36 (in-patient, serious head or spinal) | Only if in-patient or a listed injury | Only if hospital admission or listed (e.g. skull, spinal) | Not reportable unless hospitalised; always recordable (1904.7(b)(7)) | Specified injury unless finger, thumb or toe |
| Uncontrolled release of substance, gas, steam | Dangerous incident | Notifiable | Notifiable incident | Not reportable (record any injuries) | Dangerous occurrence if in Schedule 2 (e.g. flammable release para 26, hazardous escape para 27, biological agent para 10) |
| Structural or excavation collapse | Dangerous incident | Notifiable | Notifiable incident | Not reportable | Dangerous occurrence (structure over 5 t or falsework, paras 23, 24; scaffold over 5 m, para 18) |

# Industry Risk Reference Pack

Purpose: give a safety leader (or an AI skill acting for one) a fast, defensible starting point for any of 12 industries: what hurts people most often, what kills them, the critical controls that matter, how to prove those controls are working, and what regulators are looking at.

How to use it:
- "Frequent" and "fatal" are different questions with different answers. Do not let the frequent list (strains, slips) crowd out the fatal list in reports, risk registers or audit plans.
- Critical controls are written as things you can walk up to and check. Each one has a "Verify" line: who checks, how often, and what evidence exists afterwards. If the evidence does not exist, treat the control as "Not verified" (see risk-method.md).
- Frequencies are defaults for a mid-sized operation. Tighten them for high-exposure sites or after an incident.
- Stats are quoted with source and year. Australian figures are Safe Work Australia (SWA) unless stated. Do not mix rates across countries; definitions differ.

## Cross-industry baseline (use for context in any report)

- Australia, 2024: 188 workers killed by traumatic work injury, 1.3 per 100,000 workers. Mechanisms: vehicle incidents 42% (79), falls from height 13% (24), hit by moving objects 9% (17). Most deaths by industry: transport, postal and warehousing 54; agriculture, forestry and fishing 44; construction 37. Highest fatality rates: agriculture, forestry and fishing 13.7; transport, postal and warehousing 7.4; mining 3.4 per 100,000. (SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025)
- Australia, 2023-24 (preliminary): 146,700 serious claims. Body stressing 34.5%, falls/trips/slips 21.8%, hit by moving objects 16.0%, mental stress 11.5%. Mental health conditions were 12.0% of serious claims with a median 35.7 weeks lost vs 7.4 weeks for all claims. Highest serious claim frequency rates (per million hours): agriculture 10.0, public administration 9.6, manufacturing 9.4, health care and social assistance 9.4, construction 9.3, transport 9.0; all-industry 6.8. (SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025)
- Australian WHS Strategy 2023-2033: target to reduce traumatic injury fatalities by 30%. Named priority industries (70% of fatalities, 61% of serious claims): agriculture, construction, transport, manufacturing, health care and social assistance, public administration and safety. Named hazards include vehicles, falls, being hit by moving objects, body stressing, psychosocial risk, silica and other respiratory disease.
- USA, 2024: 5,070 fatal work injuries, 3.3 per 100,000 FTE. Transportation incidents 1,937 (38.2%), falls/slips/trips 844, contact with objects/equipment 756, violence 733 (470 homicides). Construction and extraction occupations 1,032 deaths; heavy truck drivers 798. (BLS CFOI, released Feb 2026)
- Great Britain, 2025/26 provisional: 126 worker deaths. Falls from height 31, struck by moving vehicle 24, struck by moving object 21. Construction 25, agriculture/forestry/fishing 22, manufacturing 18, transport and storage 15, waste 6. RIDDOR non-fatal 2024/25: slips/trips/falls same level 30%, handling/lifting/carrying 17%, struck by moving object 10%, acts of violence 10%, falls from height 8%. (HSE)
- OSHA National Emphasis Programs active as of Aug 2026: Warehousing and Distribution Center Operations (CPL 03-00-026, revised 31 Jul 2026), Heat (CPL 03-00-024, revised Apr 2026), Hazardous Machinery/Amputations in Manufacturing (CPL 03-00-027, Jun 2025), Falls (CPL 03-00-025, May 2023), Combustible Dust, Crystalline Silica, Trenching and Excavation, Process Safety Management, Primary Metals, Hexavalent Chromium, Lead, Shipbreaking.
- WorkSafe NZ priority plans 2024-2026: priority sectors agriculture, construction, forestry, manufacturing (food product, metal fabrication, wood product), plus a permit plan for regulated high-risk work (mining, adventure activities, asbestos removal, hazardous substances, cranes, scaffolding).
- Asbestos: Australia banned all use and import of asbestos from 31 December 2003, so any building built or refurbished before 2004 may contain asbestos and needs an asbestos register or survey before work that could disturb it. In NZ, buildings built or renovated before 2000 are the common trigger.
- Australia banned the manufacture, supply, processing and installation of engineered stone benchtops, panels and slabs from 1 July 2024. The respirable crystalline silica workplace exposure standard is 0.025 mg/m3 (8-hour TWA) since 1 July 2020.

## 1. Construction (building)

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing (manual handling of materials, awkward postures, repetitive tool use), falls on the same level (housekeeping, uneven ground), hit by falling or moving objects, cuts from hand and power tools.
- Construction serious claim frequency rate 9.3 per million hours vs 6.8 all-industry (SWA, 2023-24 prelim). 37 Australian worker deaths in 2024 (SWA). GB: 25 deaths in 2025/26 provisional (HSE). US construction and extraction occupations: 1,032 deaths in 2024, 370 from falls/slips/trips (BLS).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Falls from height (unprotected edges, roofs, brittle roofs and skylights, voids and penetrations, ladders, scaffold).
2. Falling objects and structural collapse (formwork, temporary works, tilt-up panels, precast, demolition).
3. Mobile plant and vehicle interaction (excavators, telehandlers, reversing trucks).
4. Cranes and lifting (suspended loads, crane overturn, contact with powerlines).
5. Electrical (contact with overhead or underground services, temporary supply).
6. Trench and excavation collapse.
7. Silica and asbestos exposure (long latency, but the largest occupational disease killers in the sector).

**Critical controls and verification**
- Falls from height
  - Edge protection or scaffold meeting AS/NZS 4994 / AS/NZS 1576 installed before work at the edge. Verify: supervisor daily pre-start check; competent scaffolder handover certificate and scaff tag inspected every 30 days and after weather or alteration.
  - Penetrations and voids covered with secured, load-rated, labelled covers. Verify: daily walk by supervisor; weekly HSE inspection with photo evidence.
  - Elevating work platforms used in place of ladders for work over 2 m, with operator licence/VOC and harness where required. Verify: VOC records audited monthly; pre-start checklist per shift.
- Falling objects and structural collapse
  - Temporary works designed and certified by a competent engineer, with a hold point before loading or removal. Verify: signed temporary works register; engineer's inspection certificate before pour or strip.
  - Exclusion zones under overhead work and lifts, physically barricaded. Verify: supervisor at each lift; spot check by HSE weekly.
- Mobile plant and pedestrian interaction
  - Physical separation (hard barriers, fenced pedestrian walkways) between mobile plant routes and people. Verify: traffic management plan reviewed at each site layout change; weekly inspection against plan with photos.
  - Spotter or traffic controller for reversing and any plant movement in shared areas; no-go zones around plant marked. Verify: daily pre-start; monthly field observation.
  - Plant fitted with reversing camera/proximity detection, seat belts, ROPS/FOPS as specified. Verify: plant entry inspection before first use; pre-start each shift.
- Cranes and lifting
  - Lift plan for every critical lift (tandem, near powerlines, over occupied areas, above set percentage of rated capacity) signed by lift supervisor. Verify: lift plan on file before lift; random audit of 10% of critical lifts monthly.
  - Crane outrigger ground bearing assessed and pads used; annual crane inspection and logbook current. Verify: logbook check at mobilisation; HSE audit monthly.
- Electrical services
  - Services located (dial before you dig plus potholing/non-destructive excavation) before breaking ground; permit to dig. Verify: permit file with locator report; supervisor sign-off.
  - Overhead powerline no-go zones and spotters per state framework; tiger tails or insulation where required. Verify: daily pre-start; network operator permit on file.
  - RCDs on all temporary supply, tested and tagged. Verify: RCD push test monthly, test and tag at intervals per AS/NZS 3760/3012.
- Trench collapse
  - Benching, battering or shoring/shields designed for depth and soil for any trench over 1.5 m. Verify: competent person inspection each shift and after rain; record in excavation register.
- Silica
  - Engineering control at source (on-tool water suppression or extraction with H-class vacuum) for cutting, grinding, chasing. Verify: weekly tool check; periodic air monitoring against 0.025 mg/m3; health monitoring records.
  - Fit-tested RPE for residual exposure. Verify: annual fit test records; spot check of RPE condition.

**Lead indicators (construction)**
- % of high-risk activities with a current, task-specific SWMS that matches observed work (field verification, not paperwork count).
- Critical control verification completion rate (falls, plant, lifting, services) and % of verifications finding a failed control.
- Temporary works hold points signed off before loading.
- Subcontractor pre-start attendance and SWMS review rate.
- Number of high-potential near misses reported per 200,000 hours (rising is often healthy early on).
- Close-out rate of overdue high-risk inspection actions within agreed time.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: construction is a national priority industry; engineered stone ban; silica, falls, mobile plant. Many states run falls and silica blitzes.
- NZ: falls from height, falling objects, vehicles and moving objects, silica, welding fume, wood dust, asbestos; managing overlapping duties on complex sites.
- UK: HSE "Work Right" dust inspection campaigns (RCS, wood dust, asbestos) on construction sites.
- US: Falls NEP (CPL 03-00-025), Silica NEP, Trenching and Excavation NEP, Heat NEP.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- High job demands and long hours driven by programme pressure; fatigue.
- Poor support from transient supervision; remote or isolated work on small sites.
- Bullying, harassment (including sexual harassment of women in trades), conflict between trades.
- Job insecurity and poor organisational change at project wind-down.
- Traumatic events after serious incidents; known high suicide risk among construction workers.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; BLS CFOI 2024; HSE fatals overview; WorkSafe NZ priority plans 2024-2026; OSHA NEP list; https://www.citation.co.uk/safety-alert-hse-launches-inspection-crackdown-on-dust-in-construction/

## 2. Civil and infrastructure

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing (manual handling of pipe, kerb, formwork), slips and trips on uneven ground, hit by moving objects, hand injuries from tools and rigging.
- Reported within SWA "Construction" division (heavy and civil engineering). Civil-specific national rates not separately published in the Key Statistics.

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Mobile plant interaction and rollover (excavators, rollers, graders, haul trucks, plant on batters).
2. Road traffic in live corridors (worker struck by public vehicle).
3. Trench and excavation collapse; engulfment.
4. Underground and overhead services strikes (gas, HV, water).
5. Lifting (pipe, precast, bridge beams).
6. Working near or over water; confined spaces (pits, sewers, culverts).

**Critical controls and verification**
- Mobile plant
  - Physical separation and exclusion zones between plant and ground workers; positive communication (radio or hand signal protocol) before entering a plant's zone. Verify: daily pre-start; weekly supervisor observation recorded.
  - Rollover protection and seat belts on all plant; interlock or seat belt alarm where fitted. Verify: plant entry inspection; monthly plant audit.
  - Slope and edge limits for plant documented and delineated (bunding/windrows at edges). Verify: engineer or supervisor check at each change of work face.
- Live traffic
  - Traffic guidance scheme designed by an accredited person and implemented as drawn; speed reduction; positive protection (concrete or water-filled barriers, truck-mounted attenuators) on high-speed roads. Verify: TGS implementation check at set-up and every shift; road authority audits; photo record.
- Trenching
  - Shoring/shields or benching designed for depth and ground type; spoil set back from edge. Verify: competent person inspection each shift and after rain or vibration.
- Services
  - Locate (DBYD/BYDA), pothole to expose before mechanical excavation within tolerance zone; permit to dig. Verify: permit and locator report on file before dig.
  - Asset owner permits and isolation for HV or gas work. Verify: copy of access permit; isolation confirmation.
- Confined spaces
  - Entry permit, atmospheric testing before and during entry, standby person, rescue plan and equipment on site. Verify: permit record with gas readings; gas detector bump test log; rescue drill annually.

**Lead indicators (civil)**
- Traffic management audits completed and % non-conformances on live corridors.
- Service strikes and near strikes per km excavated (and % where potholing was skipped).
- Plant-pedestrian interaction near misses reported.
- Confined space permits audited, % fully compliant.
- Critical lift plans in place for 100% of critical lifts.

**Regulator focus**
- Same as construction in Australia and NZ. US Trenching and Excavation NEP (CPL 02-00-161). Road authorities also audit traffic management on their networks.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Long hours, shift and night work, remote camps and fly-in fly-out rosters; fatigue driving home.
- Aggression from road users toward traffic controllers.
- Remote or isolated work; poor support on dispersed sites.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; OSHA NEP list; WorkSafe NZ priority plans 2024-2026.

## 3. Manufacturing (general)

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing (lifting, repetitive tasks), hit by moving objects, caught in machinery, cuts and lacerations, slips and trips.
- Manufacturing serious claim frequency rate 9.4 per million hours, among the highest (SWA, 2023-24 prelim). GB: 18 deaths in manufacturing 2025/26 provisional (HSE).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Machinery entanglement, crushing and amputation (guarding defeated or absent, maintenance on live plant).
2. Mobile plant (forklifts) and pedestrian interaction.
3. Falls from height (mezzanines, roofs, ladders on plant).
4. Falling objects (racking, stored materials, suspended loads).
5. Hazardous substances and fire/explosion (combustible dust, flammable liquids, toxic gas).
6. Occupational disease: silica, welding fume, isocyanates, noise.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Machinery
  - Fixed or interlocked guarding preventing access to dangerous parts; interlocks tested. Verify: operator pre-start each shift; documented interlock function test at set interval (for example monthly); guarding audit quarterly by engineer or HSE.
  - Isolation and lockout (personal danger locks, verified zero energy "try" step) for all maintenance, cleaning and jam clearing. Verify: field audit of 5 isolations per month; isolation procedure per machine on file.
  - Safety-rated control systems (light curtains, e-stops) maintained and tested. Verify: e-stop and safety function test records.
- Forklifts and pedestrians
  - Separated pedestrian walkways with physical barriers; pedestrian exclusion zones in loading docks and aisles. Verify: monthly traffic management walk with photo evidence.
  - Speed limiting, blue lights or proximity detection; seat belts worn. Verify: telematics reports weekly; pre-start each shift.
  - Licensed (HRW LF) operators only, with access control (key or card). Verify: licence register reconciled monthly.
- Falls and falling objects
  - Racking designed, installed and inspected per AS 4084, load signs present. Verify: weekly visual check by trained person; annual inspection by competent person.
  - Fixed platforms and guardrails for routine access at height. Verify: quarterly inspection.
- Hazardous substances, fire, explosion
  - Combustible dust: dust collection, housekeeping to defined limits, explosion venting/isolation per hazard analysis. Verify: housekeeping inspection each shift; annual dust hazard analysis review.
  - Exposure monitoring against WES and health monitoring where required. Verify: monitoring reports against WES; health monitoring records.

**Lead indicators (manufacturing)**
- Guarding and interlock tests completed on schedule and % failed.
- Isolation audits completed and % fully compliant.
- Forklift pedestrian near misses and telematics overspeed/impact events.
- Overdue preventive maintenance on safety-critical equipment.
- Management of change completed before plant modifications.
- Exposure monitoring results trending toward 50% of WES.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: manufacturing is a national priority industry; silica; machinery guarding.
- NZ: machinery entrapment, falling objects, vehicle strikes, hazardous substances (silica, welding fume, toxic metals, wood dust), falls from height. Food product, metal fabrication and wood product sub-sectors.
- US: Hazardous Machinery (Amputations in Manufacturing) NEP CPL 03-00-027 (2025), Combustible Dust NEP, Primary Metals NEP, Silica, Lead, Hexavalent Chromium.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Shift work and fatigue; low job control on paced production lines; repetitive monotonous work.
- Poor organisational change (restructures, automation); job insecurity.
- Conflict and bullying on floor teams; poor supervisor support.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; HSE fatals overview; WorkSafe NZ priority plans; OSHA NEP list.

## 4. Food and beverage manufacturing

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Manual handling and lifting (listed in every food sub-sector by HSE), slips and trips on wet floors (in drinks, about half of injuries are slips and half trips per HSE), struck by objects, falls from height, machinery, workplace transport (forklifts and vehicles at loading bays).
- Knife cuts (meat and poultry processing), repetitive strain (packing lines), cold stress (chillers, freezers), burns (cooking, CIP chemicals).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Machinery (mixers, slicers, conveyors, palletisers) during cleaning and sanitation with plant live.
2. Forklift and vehicle interaction at docks and in yards.
3. Confined spaces (silos, tanks, vats) and engulfment.
4. Ammonia refrigeration release.
5. Combustible dust explosions (flour, sugar, starch).
6. Falls from height (tanker tops, mezzanines).

**Critical controls and verification**
- Machinery during sanitation
  - Lockout/isolation for all cleaning that requires access inside guards, including night sanitation crews and contractors. Verify: isolation audits on night shift monthly; isolation points labelled.
  - Interlocked guards; no cleaning of live machines except by engineered means (CIP, extended nozzles from outside guard). Verify: guard interlock tests; observation of sanitation crew.
- Docks and yards
  - Vehicle restraint or dock lock / key control to prevent drive-away during loading. Verify: daily dock check; key log.
  - Pedestrian exclusion from loading zones. Verify: monthly traffic walk.
- Confined spaces
  - Permit to enter, isolation of all inlets (positive isolation), gas test, standby and rescue. Verify: permit audit; gas detector calibration records.
- Ammonia
  - Leak detection with alarms and automatic ventilation; emergency shutdown; relief valves in test date. Verify: detector calibration records; relief valve register; annual emergency drill.
- Combustible dust
  - Dust hazard analysis, housekeeping standard, explosion protection, ignition source control. Verify: shift housekeeping checks; annual DHA review.

**Lead indicators**
- Sanitation isolation audit compliance (night shift specifically).
- Ammonia detector calibration and relief valve tests on schedule.
- Slip incidents per area, linked to floor cleaning and drainage maintenance completion.
- Ergonomic risk assessments completed for packing lines; job rotation adherence.
- Agency/labour hire induction completion before first shift.

**Regulator focus**
- NZ: food product manufacturing is a named manufacturing sub-sector priority.
- US: Amputations NEP, Combustible Dust NEP, Process Safety Management NEP (ammonia systems above threshold).
- Australia: within manufacturing priority industry.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Paced, repetitive work with low job control; shift and night work.
- High proportion of labour hire and migrant workers: language barriers, poor support, fear of reporting.
- Cold and noisy environments (poor physical environment).

Sources: https://www.hse.gov.uk/Food/industries/index.htm ; WorkSafe NZ priority plans; OSHA NEP list.

## 5. Warehousing, logistics and transport

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing (lifting, loading, unloading, order picking), falls from vehicles and docks, slips and trips, hit by moving objects (pallets, cages), vehicle crashes.
- Transport, postal and warehousing: 54 Australian worker deaths in 2024, the most of any industry; fatality rate 7.4 per 100,000; serious claim frequency 9.0 per million hours (SWA). US: transportation incidents were 38.2% of all work deaths in 2024; heavy truck drivers 798 deaths (BLS).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Road crashes (heavy vehicle, fatigue, speed, distraction).
2. Forklift and mobile plant interaction with pedestrians; forklift tip-over.
3. Struck by or crushed between vehicles in yards and docks (reversing, coupling/uncoupling).
4. Falls from height (trailer decks, loads, racking).
5. Falling loads (racking collapse, unrestrained loads on opening curtains or doors).

**Critical controls and verification**
- Road
  - Fatigue management system compliant with heavy vehicle law (work/rest limits, electronic work diary where used); schedules that permit compliance. Verify: weekly review of EWD/telematics; scheduling audit quarterly.
  - In-vehicle monitoring (speed, harsh events, fatigue/distraction detection) with defined response. Verify: weekly exception reports and actions recorded.
- Forklifts and pedestrians
  - Physical separation of pedestrian walkways; no-pedestrian zones at docks and high-traffic aisles; designated crossing points. Verify: monthly traffic management walk with photos; layout review at each change.
  - Licensed operators with access control; seat belts; speed limits enforced by telematics. Verify: licence register monthly; telematics weekly.
- Yards and docks
  - Vehicle restraints or keys-in-the-office systems to stop drive-away; wheel chocks; designated driver safe zones. Verify: daily dock check log.
  - Reversing controls: spotter or reversing camera plus exclusion zone. Verify: monthly observation.
- Falling loads and racking
  - Racking inspected (AS 4084): weekly visual, annual competent person; damage tagged and offloaded. Verify: inspection reports and red-tag register.
  - Load restraint per Load Restraint Guide; procedure for opening curtainsiders and containers. Verify: load audits.

**Lead indicators**
- Fatigue/distraction alerts per 100,000 km and % actioned within 24 hours.
- Forklift impact events and pedestrian near misses per 10,000 forklift hours.
- Racking damage reports and time to offload red-tagged bays.
- Dock lock / vehicle restraint compliance checks.
- % of shifts with peak-volume staffing (overtime and pace as fatigue proxy).

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: transport is a national priority industry; heavy vehicle Chain of Responsibility under the Heavy Vehicle National Law (NHVR).
- US: Warehousing and Distribution Center Operations NEP, renewed 31 Jul 2026 for five years, covering warehouses, distribution centres, postal processing, parcel delivery and couriers (retail removed). Targets powered industrial vehicles, material handling and storage, walking-working surfaces, means of egress, fire protection, heat and ergonomics.
- UK: struck by moving vehicle is the second largest cause of worker deaths (24 in 2025/26 provisional).

**Psychosocial hazards**
- High job demands and pace set by algorithms or pick rates; electronic monitoring; low job control.
- Remote or isolated work (drivers); fatigue; aggression from public or customers (delivery).
- Shift work; job insecurity in contract and gig models.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; BLS CFOI 2024; https://www.aiha.org/news/260813-osha-renews-emphasis-program-targeting-warehousing-distribution-center-operations ; HSE fatals overview.

## 6. Hospitality

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Hitting objects with part of the body, body stressing, falls/trips/slips, hit by moving objects, heat/electricity/environmental (burns) were the top five mechanisms in SA hospitality claims 2016 to 2024 (ReturnToWork SA via SafeWork SA). Sales and service workforce nationally: body stressing 40.8% of serious claims vs 36.5% across all occupations; 51% of those from lifting, carrying or putting down objects (SWA).
- Knife cuts, burns and scalds in kitchens.

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Gas explosion or fire (commercial kitchen appliances, LPG cylinders).
2. Electrocution (wet environments, damaged appliances).
3. Violence (late-night venues, licensed premises).
4. Falls from height (cellars, ladders for storage, signage).
5. Vehicle or forklift interaction in loading areas; delivery driving.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Gas and fire
  - Gas appliances installed and serviced by licensed gasfitter; flame failure devices; gas isolation accessible. Verify: service records annually; monthly manager check.
  - Kitchen exhaust and duct cleaning on schedule; fire suppression system serviced. Verify: cleaning certificates; six-monthly service per AS 1851.
- Electrical
  - RCD protection on all circuits; test and tag of portable appliances. Verify: RCD test log; tagging records.
- Violence
  - Security staff and procedures on high-risk nights, duress alarms, CCTV, lighting, no lone close-ups. Verify: duress alarm test monthly; incident review weekly.
- Falls into cellars
  - Cellar trapdoor guarded when open. Verify: daily opening checklist.

**Lead indicators**
- Induction completion for new and young workers before first shift.
- Slip-resistant footwear compliance and floor cleaning schedule adherence.
- Aggression incidents reported per 1,000 trading hours.
- Gas and fire system service completion on time.
- Rostered break compliance and shifts over 10 hours.

**Regulator focus**
- SafeWork SA hospitality compliance campaign 2025-26: body stressing, slips/trips/falls, hit by moving objects, chemical safety, and mental stress from patron violence and aggression; targeting businesses with many young, new and non-English-speaking workers.
- Accommodation and food services is not in the SWA national priority list but sales and service workers are a focus of SWA data releases.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Customer and patron aggression; sexual harassment (high-risk industry with young, casual workforce).
- High demands at peak service; long irregular hours; low job control.
- Poor support (young supervisors), wage and roster insecurity (inadequate reward, organisational justice).

Sources: https://www.safework.sa.gov.au/enforcement/compliance-campaigns-2025-26/hospitality ; https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/media-centre/news/new-data-reveals-whs-risks-facing-australias-sales-and-service-workforce

## 7. Mining and quarrying

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing, hit by moving objects, falls, slips and trips (including from mobile plant access ladders), hand injuries in maintenance.
- Mining fatality rate 3.4 per 100,000 workers in 2024, third highest (SWA).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks (aligned to principal hazards in NSW WHS (Mines and Petroleum Sites) Regulation)**
1. Vehicle and mobile plant collisions and interactions (light vehicle vs heavy vehicle, haul road edges).
2. Ground or strata failure (underground falls of ground, highwall and pit wall failure).
3. Inrush or inundation of water or materials.
4. Fire or explosion (underground coal gas and dust, tyre fires, explosives).
5. Air quality and airborne contaminants (coal and silica dust, diesel particulate).
6. Shafts and winding, gas outbursts, spontaneous combustion, subsidence (site-dependent).
7. Isolation failure during maintenance; falls from height on fixed plant; electrical.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Vehicles and mobile plant
  - Segregation of light and heavy vehicles (separate roads, positive communication, call-up procedure for entry to heavy vehicle zones). Verify: shift supervisor checks; monthly traffic management audit.
  - Windrows (bunds) at least half the wheel height of the largest vehicle at road and dump edges. Verify: road inspection each shift; surveyed audit quarterly.
  - Collision avoidance / proximity detection and fatigue detection fitted and functional. Verify: system health reports daily; monthly analytics review.
- Ground control
  - Geotechnical design with ground support standards, inspection by competent person each shift, exclusion zones for unsupported ground. Verify: geotech inspection records; monitoring data (radar, prisms) reviewed per TARP.
- Inrush
  - Mapping of old workings and water bodies; probe drilling ahead of mining near hazards. Verify: survey records; probe hole logs.
- Fire and explosion
  - Gas monitoring with automatic trip, ventilation control, stone dusting (underground coal), explosives management. Verify: gas monitoring alarms tested; dust sampling records; ventilation surveys.
- Isolation
  - Personal isolation locks, try step, group isolation box. Verify: monthly isolation field audits.

**Lead indicators**
- Critical control verifications completed and % found failed (by principal hazard).
- Heavy/light vehicle interaction near misses and proximity alerts per operating hour.
- Fatigue alerts per operator hour and response compliance.
- TARP triggers on geotechnical monitoring and response times.
- Overdue safety-critical maintenance (brakes, fire suppression, gas detection).
- Dust exposure results relative to WES.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: state mines regulators (NSW Resources Regulator, Resources Safety and Health Queensland, WA DEMIRS) with principal hazard management plans and incident notification; NSW quarterly safety reports emphasise vehicle collisions and mobile plant. Silica and coal mine dust lung disease.
- US: MSHA (not covered by OSHA NEPs).
- ICMM critical control management originated in mining and is the industry benchmark.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- FIFO/DIDO rosters, long shifts, remote isolated camps, separation from family.
- Sexual harassment in camps and male-dominated sites (subject of WA parliamentary inquiry "Enough is Enough", 2022).
- Fatigue; high demands at shutdown; poor organisational change in cyclical downturns.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; https://www.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nsw/consol_reg/whasapsr2014563/s5.html ; https://www.resources.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-08/fact-sheet-rovoa-collision-of-vehicles-or-mobile-plant.pdf ; ICMM CCM guide.

## 8. Healthcare and aged care

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing from patient and resident handling: 47.6% of serious claims in the nursing, care and support workforce on average since 2012-13 vs 37.8% for all occupations; serious musculoskeletal claim rate 2.3 times the national average in 2022-23 (SWA).
- Being assaulted: serious claim frequency rate from assault 6.5 times higher than all other occupations, and rising (SWA).
- Slips and trips, needlestick and sharps, mental stress. Health care and social assistance serious claim frequency 9.4 per million hours (SWA, 2023-24).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Occupational violence (patients, residents, family members; community and home visits).
2. Road crashes (community and home care workers driving between clients).
3. Infectious disease (bloodborne, airborne, pandemic).
4. Hazardous drugs and chemicals (cytotoxics, anaesthetic gases).
5. Suicide risk among clinicians (psychosocial).
6. For patients: falls, but that is clinical risk, keep separate.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Occupational violence
  - Risk flagging of known aggressive patients/clients shared at handover and in scheduling. Verify: audit of flags in records monthly.
  - Duress alarms (fixed and personal) tested, with defined security/code response. Verify: monthly alarm test; response time from drills.
  - Environmental design (two exits in interview rooms, secured medication areas, controlled access). Verify: annual environmental audit.
  - Home visit risk assessment and check-in/check-out system for lone workers. Verify: weekly report of missed check-ins and escalations.
- Patient handling
  - Mechanical aids (ceiling hoists, slide sheets, height-adjustable beds) available at the point of care with a no-lift policy; individual mobility plans. Verify: equipment availability audit; maintenance records; observation audits.
- Infection
  - Vaccination program, sharps safety devices and disposal, airborne precautions with fit-tested P2/N95. Verify: vaccination register; fit test records; sharps injury trends.

**Lead indicators**
- % of patients/residents with mobility plans current.
- Hoist and equipment availability (out-of-service counts).
- Code grey/black events per 1,000 bed days and % with post-incident debrief.
- Staffing ratio shortfalls and missed breaks.
- Duress alarm tests completed; lone worker check-in compliance.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: health care and social assistance is a national priority industry; occupational violence and psychosocial risk. Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission oversees aged care standards.
- UK: violence and aggression in health and social care; MSDs.
- US: no current healthcare NEP; workplace violence is cited under the General Duty Clause.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- High job demands, understaffing, and emotional demands.
- Traumatic events (deaths, resuscitation, abuse).
- Violence and aggression; sexual harassment by patients.
- Shift work and fatigue; poor organisational change (restructures, funding changes); moral injury.

Sources: https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/profile/whs-profile-nursing-care-support-workforce ; SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025.

## 9. Property and facilities management

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Slips, trips and falls (cleaners, maintenance), body stressing (cleaning, waste handling), cuts, chemical exposure.
- No separate SWA industry rate is published; cleaners and maintenance trades fall under several ANZSIC divisions.

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Falls from height (roof access, plant rooms, ladders, skylights, building facade access).
2. Electrical (switchboards, contractors working live).
3. Contractor activity: hot work fire, confined spaces (tanks, pits), lifts and escalator maintenance.
4. Asbestos-containing materials disturbed during refurbishment.
5. Legionella from cooling towers (public and worker health).
6. Vehicle interaction in car parks and loading docks.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Roof and height access
  - Certified anchor points and static lines, guardrailed roof walkways, compliant fixed ladders. Verify: height safety system inspection and certification every 12 months per AS/NZS 1891.4; register on site.
  - Permit to work for roof access. Verify: permit log audited monthly.
- Contractor management
  - Pre-qualification (licences, insurance, SWMS), site induction, permit system for high-risk work (hot work, confined spaces, electrical, roof). Verify: contractor register audit quarterly; spot checks of active permits.
- Electrical
  - No live work policy; isolation and lockout by licensed electricians; switchboard thermography. Verify: permit record; annual thermography report.
- Asbestos
  - Asbestos register and management plan current (review at least every 5 years in model WHS Regs), checked before any refurbishment. Verify: register review date; evidence register issued with work orders.
- Legionella
  - Cooling tower risk management plan, monthly microbial testing, independent audit per state rules. Verify: test results and audit certificate.

**Lead indicators**
- % of high-risk contractor jobs with verified permit and SWMS.
- Height safety certification currency across portfolio.
- Asbestos register currency per site.
- Cooling tower test results within limits.
- Contractor induction completion before access.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: asbestos, falls, contractor control under overlapping duties (s46 model WHS Act consultation, cooperation and coordination). Cooling tower rules are state public health regulation.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Lone and after-hours work (cleaners, security); aggression from tenants or the public.
- Low job control and poor support for outsourced workforces; job insecurity at contract rollover.

Sources: SWA model WHS framework; https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/identify-assess-and-control-hazards/managing-risks

## 10. Agriculture

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing, falls (from vehicles, ladders), hit by moving objects, animal-related injuries (kicks, crush, bites), machinery cuts.
- Agriculture, forestry and fishing: highest fatality rate of any industry in Australia, 13.7 per 100,000 (44 deaths, 2024) and highest serious claim frequency 10.0 per million hours (SWA). Farmsafe Australia Safer Farms report: 74 on-farm deaths in 2024, highest in 20 years; side-by-side vehicles 14, quad bikes 10, tractors 8. GB: 22 deaths 2025/26 provisional (HSE).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Vehicle rollover and crashes (quads, side-by-sides, tractors, utes).
2. Machinery entanglement (PTO shafts, augers, balers).
3. Animal strikes and crush (cattle yards, bulls).
4. Falling objects (bales, trees in forestry).
5. Drowning (dams, irrigation channels).
6. Chemicals (pesticides) and confined spaces (silos).

**Critical controls and verification**
- Vehicles
  - Right vehicle for the task (side-by-side or ute instead of quad where suitable); rollover protection (OPD/ROPS) and seat belts on side-by-sides and tractors; helmets on quads. Verify: vehicle register with ROPS/OPD fitted; monthly check by farm manager.
  - Child exclusion from operating vehicles and machinery areas. Verify: farm rules signed; observation.
- Machinery
  - PTO guards intact, shut-down before clearing blockages. Verify: pre-use check per machine; guard condition at service.
- Livestock
  - Yard design separating handlers from animals (races, crushes, escape gaps). Verify: annual yard inspection.
- Remote work
  - Check-in system or GPS/satellite device for lone workers. Verify: missed check-in log.

**Lead indicators**
- % vehicles with ROPS/OPD and seat belts.
- Pre-use machinery checks completed.
- Lone worker check-in compliance.
- Seasonal worker induction (language-appropriate) before start.
- Chemical storage audits.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: agriculture is a national priority industry; quad bike Safety Standard (mandatory OPD and stability requirements since 2021 under consumer law).
- NZ: agriculture priority (dairy, sheep and beef): vehicle rollovers and crashes, falling objects, machinery entrapment, animal strikes and bites, hazardous substances; "Farm Without Harm". Forestry priority: struck or trapped by trees and logs; vehicle rollovers.
- US: Heat NEP covers outdoor agricultural work.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Remote and isolated work; financial stress and drought; long hours at harvest.
- Family business conflict; fatigue; high rural suicide rates.
- For seasonal and migrant workers: poor support, accommodation conditions, harassment and exploitation risk.

Sources: SWA Key WHS Statistics 2025; https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9022602/farm-fatalities-leap-as-new-culprit-surpasses-quad-bikes-tractors/ ; WorkSafe NZ priority plans 2024-2026.

## 11. Utilities and energy (electricity, gas, water, waste)

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing, slips and trips on uneven ground, vehicle incidents, dog bites and aggression for field workers, insect and heat exposure.
- GB: waste and recycling had 6 worker deaths in 2025/26 provisional (HSE).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Electrical contact and arc flash (HV and LV networks, substations).
2. Falls from height (poles, towers, elevated platforms).
3. Vehicles (road travel, traffic on roadside works, refuse truck reversing).
4. Confined spaces (pits, sewers, water tanks) with toxic or oxygen-deficient atmospheres.
5. Loss of containment, fire and explosion (gas networks, process plant).
6. Trench collapse and underground services.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Electrical
  - Isolation, test for dead, earth and short-circuit before work, under an access permit issued by an authorised person. Verify: permit audits monthly; switching records.
  - Minimum approach distances to live conductors enforced, with insulated tools and live-line work only under specific accreditation. Verify: field observation; competency register.
  - Arc-rated PPE matched to incident energy calculation. Verify: arc flash study on file; PPE condition checks.
- Falls (poles, towers)
  - Pole integrity test before climbing, fall arrest 100% tie-off. Verify: test record; observation.
- Confined spaces
  - Permit, continuous gas monitoring, standby and retrieval equipment. Verify: permit audit; detector calibration.
- Process safety (gas, treatment plants)
  - Safety instrumented systems and relief devices tested; management of change. Verify: proof test records; overdue SIS tests reported (CCPS Tier 4).

**Lead indicators**
- Network access permit audits and % compliant.
- Minimum approach distance breaches reported.
- Safety-critical inspections and SIS proof tests completed on time (CCPS/API RP 754 Tier 4).
- Demands on safety systems and process deviations (Tier 3).
- Heat stress controls compliance for field crews.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: state electrical safety regulators; gas technical regulators; process safety via Major Hazard Facility rules where thresholds met.
- US: PSM NEP, Heat NEP; OSHA electric power generation, transmission and distribution standard (29 CFR 1910.269).
- EEI (Edison Electric Institute) led the SIF classification research used in investigation-method.md.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Emergency callouts and storm response: fatigue and long hours.
- Aggression from customers (meter readers, disconnection crews).
- Remote and isolated work; trauma after serious incidents.

Sources: https://www.aiche.org/sites/default/files/docs/pages/ccps_process_safety_metrics_-_v3.2.pdf ; HSE fatals overview; OSHA NEP list.

## 12. Retail

**Most frequent injury mechanisms**
- Body stressing (stocking shelves, lifting, trolleys): 40.8% of serious claims in sales and service roles; 51% of those from lifting, carrying or putting down objects (SWA). Slips and trips, hit by falling stock, cuts (box cutters).
- Customer aggression rising (SDA and retail industry surveys).

**Fatal and catastrophic risks**
1. Armed hold-up and violence.
2. Forklift and pallet jack interaction in back-of-house and warehouse-style stores.
3. Falling stock from high shelving or racking in trading hours.
4. Falls from ladders while stocking.
5. Road crashes for delivery and online fulfilment drivers.

**Critical controls and verification**
- Violence
  - Cash handling controls (time-delay safes, minimal cash), CCTV, duress alarms, counter and screen design; no lone close-up. Verify: monthly alarm test; incident review.
  - De-escalation and incident response procedure; post-incident support. Verify: training records plus incident debrief rate.
- Forklifts and stock at height
  - No forklift operation in trading areas during open hours, or physical exclusion zones. Verify: store manager daily check; operation logs.
  - Racking inspection (AS 4084) and load restraint at height (netting, beams). Verify: weekly visual; annual competent inspection.
- Ladders
  - Platform ladders with handrails only; no step stools for high stock. Verify: equipment inventory audit quarterly.

**Lead indicators**
- Aggression incidents reported per store per month (low reporting is a red flag).
- Duress alarm test completion.
- Racking inspection completion and red-tag time to offload.
- Manual handling aids availability (trolleys, lifting aids) and use.
- Young worker induction completion before first shift.

**Regulator focus**
- Australia: retail customer aggression (SafeWork SA retail sector compliance project on violence and aggression); several states legislated tougher penalties for assaulting retail workers.
- US: retail stores were removed from the warehousing NEP in the July 2026 revision.

**Psychosocial hazards**
- Customer aggression and abuse; sexual harassment.
- Peak-season demands; low job control over rosters; casual insecurity.
- Remote/isolated work in small stores and late-night trading.
